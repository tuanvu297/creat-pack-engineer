# Productizing a repo or tool

Identify the buyer and concrete job: a release team buying packaging tools,
an AI integrator buying an adapter starter, or an operator buying usable software.
For each deliverable define scope, install/reinstall procedure, supported
environments, examples, acceptance criteria, upgrade path, support boundary and
evidence. Provide a reproducible source checkpoint and customer-facing README.

Before a public commercial release, resolve ownership of included material,
dependency/asset license inventory, selected customer license, support/update
policy, refund/service terms where applicable, and provider costs/access.
These are user business decisions; do not choose a price or grant public
redistribution rights on the user's behalf. Mark unknowns as release blockers
for sale, not as failures of a local prototype.

Versioning: use semantic versions, record changes and preserve prior receipts.
Do not market a template's local demo as a live provider integration, or one
Windows test as cross-platform verification. Separate proposed product claims
from demonstrated behavior. Publication and paid services need their own scope.
